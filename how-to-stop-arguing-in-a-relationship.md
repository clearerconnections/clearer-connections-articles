# How to Stop Arguing in a Relationship

After a fight, you may both feel upset. Maybe you feel like you weren't heard, or you just feel drained. While fighting is natural, there are ways to slow down your fights. Allow yourselves to slow down and listen to each other, and work on your problem as a couple. For more ideas, read [how to improve communication in a relationship](https://clearerconnections.com/how-to-improve-communication-in-a-relationship/).

Here are some tips on how to stop fighting.

![A couple having a calm conversation at home](https://clearerconnections.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/couple-calm-home-conversation-2e4225d2-1024x585.jpg)

## Stop Yourself From Getting Into A Fight

When you're angry, you may not know how to express yourself properly. You may yell at your partner or not allow them to finish their sentence.

Allow yourself to know when it's time to take a break. Maybe you're getting louder or you aren't listening to your partner. Tell your partner that you need some time. Make sure you tell them when you will come back to the conversation.

"I need 20 minutes to cool down; then we can come back to this."

Remember, your break is not to punish your partner or avoid the problem. Take that time to cool down and come back.

## Stick To One Problem

Your argument can escalate if you add old problems to the current problem. Try to focus on what your current problem is.

Don't go off on what your partner did wrong. Explain what happened and how it affected you. By doing this, you're both working on the same problem.

If there is another problem that you need to discuss, make sure to write it down or remember to bring it up at another time.

## Say "I" Statements

If you use "you" statements, it can put your partner on the defensive. Tell your partner how you feel.

"I feel that you don't listen to me when you cut me off. Please listen to what I'm saying and then reply."

Using an "I" statement doesn't mean you can hide what you want to say. Just state how you feel and give your partner the opportunity to share how they feel as well.

## Listen To Your Partner

Listen to what your partner is saying and try to understand, even if you feel they are wrong.

Repeat what they said to you. "So you feel that I didn't include you when I made those plans. Is that correct?"

You don't have to agree with everything your partner says. Show that you listened to them by saying something such as, "I understand why you got upset."

## Don't Call Names Or Use The Words Always And Never

Don't use words such as always and never. Calling your partner names, threats, ridiculing them, or telling them things that you know will hurt them can cause serious problems in your relationship.

Tell your partner what you want to address, not what type of person they are.

"I didn't like that you left the dishes in the sink overnight."

If you or your partner begin name-calling or using those types of words, stop talking and agree to come back when you're ready.

## What Are They Trying To Tell You?

Maybe there is something more that your partner is trying to tell you. There is always more to an argument than meets the eye. Maybe they feel like you don't care about them or that you aren't including them.

Ask each other what they want out of this situation.

"How can I support you in this situation?"

"What do you need from me?"

"What do you want me to understand about this situation?"

Once you understand what they need, you can both work on solving your problem.

## Come To A Conclusion

There is no need for someone to win in an argument. Once you both know what the other person needs, come up with a solution.

Make sure that you come to a conclusion that you both can live with. If you say we will communicate better, how are you going to do that?

Come up with solutions that work for you both. Maybe check in with each other before making plans for the weekend. Or maybe your argument was about housework, and you can split it differently.

Sometimes there is no instant solution to your problem. Try to come up with something and give it a week. Then reassess if it worked. You can also read these [practical ways to work through relationship problems](https://clearerconnections.com/relationship-problems/).

## How To Fix Things After An Argument

Even if you follow all these steps, things can still go wrong. If you said something you know wasn't nice, apologize. Make sure you tell them what you did wrong.

"I'm sorry I yelled at you. That prevented us from talking about our problem. I will take a break when I feel this way."

Also, remind yourself of what went right. Thank your partner for trying to work things out with you. And come back to what you're both working on when you're ready.

## When Should I Seek Help?

If you find that you're constantly fighting about the same thing, or you're unable to have a conversation without fighting, it may be helpful to speak to a couples therapist. Or, if you need to speak to someone about what you need from your relationship, you can seek help on your own.

If your arguments consist of violence, please reach out for help that will keep you and your family safe.
